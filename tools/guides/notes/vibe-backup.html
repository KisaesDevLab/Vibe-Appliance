First-time setup (~10 minutes, once). After enabling, open the app from its card link:

  1. Create a Backblaze account at backblaze.com (B2 Cloud Storage) and put the firm's payment card on it — the storage bill is the firm's.
  2. In Backblaze: App Keys → Generate New Master Application Key.
  3. In this app's wizard: enter the firm name and that master key. The wizard creates the bucket, the immutability lock, and restricted keys itself — you never touch the Backblaze console again, and the master key is used once and never stored.
  4. Print the Recovery Kit and put it in the safe. Type the six words from the kit back into the app. Backups begin only after this — a backup whose passphrase is lost is not a backup, so the system refuses to start without the confirmed kit.

The Recovery Kit is the master key to all firm and client data. Safe or safe-deposit box, two named custodians. Losing it is a security incident. When the app announces a new kit (once a year), print and confirm the new one before shredding the old. Print the restore runbook too (admin console → Apps header → restore runbook PDF) and keep it with the kit — its disaster tier is read on a day the console is unreachable.

What it protects — and doesn't. It protects the Vibe appliance: databases, documents, settings, and keys, nightly, verified by a monthly restore drill. It does not back up workstations, office file servers, email, or other tax software — those need their own backup.

Coverage today — keep Duplicati configured too. Per-app databases and key material are captured as each Vibe app ships its backup contract, and that rollout is still in progress. Until the appliance's release notes say otherwise, treat this app and the Duplicati backup (appliance guide, "Set up backups") as a pair, not a replacement.

Day to day: nothing. The card in this app shows Last verified restore — the number that matters, because it means a restore was actually performed and every module could decrypt its data. If the card is red it says what is wrong in one sentence; the most common cause is an expired card on the Backblaze account.
